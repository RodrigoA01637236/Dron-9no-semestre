## 1. Banana/plantain production in Mexico by state, producers, smallholders, indigenous producers, marginalization

### Takeaway
Mexico grows bananas/plantains on roughly 87,000 ha in 16 states and harvests about 2.4 to 2.6 Mt a year. Chiapas, Tabasco and Veracruz produce about two thirds of it. In Chiapas and Tabasco, the two main states, most producers have almost no credit (2–4% of production units) or insurance (<1%), say high input costs are their top problem (82–85%), and many are indigenous (55% of Chiapas producers, 27% of Tabasco producers, all crops, Census 2022). That is a strong profile for a low-cost, shared detection service. I found no official count of banana producers or banana-specific ejido or smallholder shares.

### Cited Findings
**National totals**
- SADER/SENASICA (Jan 2026): Mexican producers grow 11 banana/plantain varieties on more than 87,000 ha in 16 states and harvest more than 2.6 million t a year. — [Portal Frutícola, 14 Jan 2026, citing Agricultura/SENASICA](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=609837)
- Average national production in 2018–2022 was 2.4 million t/yr, growing 2.4% a year on average. Exports over 2018–2022 were worth US$281 million: United States 74.3%, Japan 15.6%, Canada 0.2%. — [Portal Frutícola, 18 Apr 2024, citing SADER/SIAP](https://www.portalfruticola.com/noticias/2024/04/18/norteamerica-y-japon-son-los-principales-consumidores-de-platano-mexicano)

**State ranking (SIAP-based)**
- 2018–2022 average: Chiapas 686,000 t (28.1%, 1st); Tabasco 609,000 t (24.9%, 2nd); Veracruz 315,000 t (12.9%, 3rd, growing 4.4% a year). — [Portal Frutícola, Apr 2024, citing SIAP](https://www.portalfruticola.com/noticias/2024/04/18/norteamerica-y-japon-son-los-principales-consumidores-de-platano-mexicano)

Leadership between Chiapas and Tabasco changes from year to year. — [Portal Frutícola, 18 Nov 2021](https://www.portalfruticola.com/noticias/2021/11/18/mexico-pais-que-se-autobastece-de-platano/) (snippet, not opened)

### Inferences
- The census and SIAP measure area differently. INEGI 2022 counts about 30,700 ha for Tabasco and 31,600 ha for Chiapas, which together already exceed one third of SADER's national ~87,000 ha. The 2013 Tabasco figure was ~17,000 ha. The census counts any cultivated plot in a production unit, including mixed and backyard plots, so it probably captures many small, scattered growers that SIAP's commercial statistics undercount. That is the user group the project targets. Give the source with every area number.
- Credit at 2–4% and insurance under 1% mean smallholders cannot pay for weekly calendar spraying or hire private crop-scouting technicians. A shared drone service run by a cooperative, ejido or committee fits this profile.
- More than half of Chiapas producers are indigenous, and 87% of those speak an indigenous language. Two-thirds of Tabasco producers in 2022 did no pest/disease monitoring at all. The offline web app should therefore rely on icons, colour-coded maps and voice or local-language labels. Offline operation is already justified.
- Producers are old (Tabasco: 76% over 45, 27% over 65). Walking and inspecting leaves is physically demanding, which supports drone-based scouting.

The SIAP padrón, the SADER Sistema Producto Plátano and the INEGI CA 2022 tabulados by crop and tenure should hold one, but I could not open them: infosiap.siap.gob.mx did not resolve from this environment, and the Chiapas CEIEG agriculture notebook PDF is image-only.

## 2. Black Sigatoka impact: yield loss, spray frequency, cost, share of production cost, environmental and health effects, fungicide resistance

### Takeaway
Uncontrolled Black Sigatoka cuts yield 35–50% (and up to 100% in some reports). Chiapas border producers reported losses of up to 40% in 2021. Mexican commercial plantations spray 25 to 52 times a year, and control takes roughly 35–60% of production cost. Mexican isolates already show reduced sensitivity to propiconazole (DMI/triazole) and azoxystrobin (QoI/strobilurin), even in unsprayed "rustic" fields next to intensively sprayed ones. This matters for smallholders because spraying by large neighbours erodes the tools smallholders rely on.

### Cited Findings
**Yield and quality losses**
- Without chemical control and under favourable conditions, yield can fall 35–50% and no functional leaf may remain at harvest. Infected plants ripen fruit early and unevenly, which makes it unfit for export. — [ProMusa, Black leaf streak](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)
- CICY (2018): Black Sigatoka "puede provocar entre 40 y 100 por ciento de pérdida de la producción si no se controla". — [Diario de Yucatán, 24 Jan 2018](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)
- SENASICA (16 Jun 2021): plantain producers in the Chiapas border region reported high Black Sigatoka incidence "en todas las unidades de producción" and losses of up to 40% of production, because leaf damage keeps bunches from filling. SENASICA also notes that a leaf with dense lesions "se torna negra y muere en 3-4 semanas" after first symptoms. — [SENASICA Monitor Fitosanitario 16-06-2021](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/Contenido/files/2021/junio/MonitorFitosanitario16062021_a6a0f3b3-26b8-45cd-b974-bfd014d2c182.pdf) (original story: [El Orbe, 16 Jun 2021](https://elorbe.com/seccion-politica/local/2021/06/16/plaga-de-la-sigatoka-azota-a-plataneros.html))

**Spray frequency**
- Tabasco: chemical management needs **48–52 fungicide applications a year**, mainly mancozeb, propiconazole and tridemorph. — [Agrociencia 2012 study as summarized, Scribd copy](https://es.scribd.com/document/693914784/Agroquimicos) (snippet, not opened)
- Mexico, Brazilian Journal of Microbiology 2014: "intensive management" fields are defined as those with more than 25 applications a year. — [BVS record lil-709492](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/en/lil-709492)
- Costa Rica: about 30 applications a year was normal in the 1990s and more than 50 by 2007 (Lapeyre de Bellaire et al., 2010). In commercial Cavendish and plantain, spraying can be weekly. — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)
- A Mexican Pacific-coast grower reports spraying every 8 days, more often in the rainy season. — [Portal Frutícola](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=20910) (snippet, not opened)
- CICY (2018): chemicals are used "de forma semanal". — [Diario de Yucatán 2018](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)

**Cost and share of production cost (sources conflict)**
- **Tabasco sector, 2013:** fighting Black Sigatoka cost producers on average **2 million pesos per week, about 100 million pesos per year**, which "represents 35 percent of the production costs". At the time ~17,000 ha were planted and ~2,000 ha affected. Quoted: president of Fundación Produce Tabasco (Adrián Prats Leal). The FreshPlaza headline says "100 million dollars", but the article body says pesos. — [FreshPlaza, 7 Jun 2013](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/2110011/mexico-banana-producers-lose-100-million-dollars-a-year/)
- 2012 (INIFAP/Chapingo program for Tabasco): agrochemicals for Sigatoka take up to 35% of per-hectare spending. — [Portal Frutícola, 3 May 2012](https://www.portalfruticola.com/noticias/2012/05/03/mexico-mejoraran-tecnicas-para-la-produccion-del-platano-en-tabasco/) (snippet, not opened)
- CICY (2018): control is about 50% of total production cost. — [Diario de Yucatán 2018](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)
- A researcher quoted by Portal Frutícola puts fungicides at about 60% of Mexican banana growers' costs. — [Portal Frutícola](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=20910) (snippet, not opened)
- About US$1,100/ha/yr in fungicides, and more than US$55 million national spend in 2010. The per-hectare cost comes from a 2003 source. — [UACH TecnoCiencia Chihuahua](https://revistascientificas.uach.mx/index.php/tecnociencia/article/download/594/2491/11419) (snippet; the full text returned HTTP 403)
- For comparison, Ecuador (Univ. de Cuenca): one application costs about US$139/ha by drone and about US$151/ha by small plane. — [Universidad de Cuenca thesis](https://rest-dspace.ucuenca.edu.ec/server/api/core/bitstreams/f7c3939c-64d3-44ab-90c5-0ce99780cbb2/content) (snippet, not opened)
- The Tecnológico de Costa Rica notes that transnationals with large plantations can absorb control costs easily, while small producers struggle because their profitability is low. — [Revista Tecnología en Marcha](https://revistas.tec.ac.cr/index.php/tec_marcha/article/download/15/14) (snippet, not opened)
- A Panamanian report says smallholders find it harder than technified plantations to keep controls going consistently. — [La Prensa (Panamá)](https://www.prensa.com/economia/banano-y-platano-bajo-la-sombra-de-una-nueva-amenaza-la-sigatoka-negra/) (snippet, not opened)

**Reducing fungicide use**
- A CICY-led multi-institution project (with UACh, UJAT, INIFAP, U. de Colima and UAG) cut fungicide use by up to 40% in Colima and 20% in Tabasco on cooperating farms. It ran field work in Colima, Tabasco, Chiapas and Oaxaca. — [Diario de Yucatán, 24 Jan 2018](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)

**Fungicide resistance in Mexico**
- Agrociencia (2012), isolates from Tabasco and Chiapas: propiconazole EC50 ranged from 0.0001 to 0.95 mg/L and EC95 from 0.006 to 12.51 mg/L. The resistance factor at EC95 was above **2,085×** for isolate MF-RmC2 from Finca Remedios, Teapa, Tabasco. — [ECOSUR library record](https://biblioteca.ecosur.mx/bib/52390); summary via search (snippet)
- Brazilian Journal of Microbiology (2014), Mexican fields: mean azoxystrobin EC50 was **13.25 mg/L** in rustic (unsprayed) fields and **51.58 mg/L** in intensive fields (>25 apps/yr). Propiconazole: **1.225 vs 10.01 mg/L**. Sensitivity to all fungicides fell in isolates from intensive fields. Resistance indicators also appeared in rustic-field strains, "suggesting that proximity among fields may be causing a fungus interchange". — [BVS lil-709492](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/en/lil-709492)
- Heavy reliance on systemic fungicides selects for resistant strains, so growers alternate contact and systemic products. Systemics do not clear lesions already present, so diseased tissue has to be removed by hand. — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)

**Environmental and health effects**
- Mineral oils used with fungicides interfere with photosynthesis and can be phytotoxic in hot, dry weather. Oil carriers are what made aerial spraying possible. — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)
- Occupational and environmental risks of mancozeb at aerial-spraying facilities in the Teapa banana zone, Tabasco: Tropical Journal of Pharmaceutical Research 14(9), 2015, doi 10.4314/tjpr.v14i9.23. I could not read the conclusions. — [AJOL](https://ajol.info/index.php/tjpr/article/view/123690); [Zendy](https://zendy.io/title/10.4314/tjpr.v14i9.23)
- Cunduacán, Tabasco (Contactos, UAM, 2021): a study of pesticide-related health risk factors in plantain farmers, focused on Black Sigatoka control in monoculture, found that some workers do not use personal protective measures. — [Contactos UAM-I](https://contactos.izt.uam.mx/index.php/contactos/article/download/151/100) (snippet, not opened)
- 1987: because of Black Sigatoka, the Dirección General de Sanidad Vegetal set a permanent internal quarantine ("Cuarentena Interior Permanente") to keep the disease out of banana regions where it was not yet present. — [SENASICA Monitor Fitosanitario 16-06-2021](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/Contenido/files/2021/junio/MonitorFitosanitario16062021_a6a0f3b3-26b8-45cd-b974-bfd014d2c182.pdf)

**History**
- The first Latin American record was Honduras in 1972. Mexico followed in 1980, after Guatemala (1977) and Costa Rica (1979). — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)

### Inferences
- **Correct the earlier pitch number.** The "~2 million MXN" figure most likely traces to the 2013 Tabasco statement: **2 million MXN per week for the whole Tabasco plantain sector**, about 100 million MXN a year over ~17,000 ha. It was not a per-plantation cost. Dividing gives about **5,900 MXN/ha/yr in 2013 pesos** (100,000,000 / 17,000). That is my calculation, not a published figure. The other estimate, US$1,100/ha/yr from a 2003 source, is much higher. Present a range and name both sources.
- Smallholders do not need the 25–52 calendar sprays of export plantations. They do need to know where and when disease is building so they can deleaf, prune and target the few sprays they can afford. The 2018 CICY results (20–40% less fungicide with better monitoring and management) give a credible, sourced benchmark for the impact of targeted rather than whole-field spraying.
- The 2014 finding that unsprayed rustic fields near intensive farms already carry less-sensitive strains strengthens the community case. Resistance spreads between neighbours, so fungicide stewardship coordinated across an ejido or cooperative, informed by a shared disease map, is a public good.

### Gaps
- No Mexican per-hectare control cost from 2018 or later, no current box/kg price, and no cost structure for smallholder plantain. Possible sources: SIAP "costos de producción" sheets, FIRA "agrocostos" for plátano, state SEDAFOP Tabasco.
- No Mexico-specific yield-loss figure for untreated plantain (AAB "macho") in smallholder systems. Plantain is often reported as somewhat less susceptible than Cavendish, but I found no Mexican source for that here.
- I found no reliable, documented recent complaint from Tabasco communities about aerial fungicide drift. Only occupational studies.
- The Agrociencia 2012 abstract was seen only through snippets; I did not open the full text.

## 3. How monitoring is done today and by whom; what smallholders can access; cultural management

### Takeaway
Standard Sigatoka monitoring is manual leaf-by-leaf scoring by trained technicians: the Stover youngest-leaf-spotted (YLS) count and the biological forewarning ("preaviso biológico") "estado de evolución" on leaves 2–4 of young plants, checked weekly. It is labour-intensive and largely limited to technified plantations. SENASICA runs federal campaigns for Moko and active Foc R4T surveillance, which since 2025 includes drones and satellites. I found no current federal or state campaign specifically for Black Sigatoka. In 2022, 59–65% of production units in Tabasco and Chiapas did no pest/disease monitoring of any kind.

### Cited Findings
**Methods**
- **Youngest Leaf Spotted (Stover 1971):** the youngest leaf with at least 10 necrotic lesions, counted from the newest leaf. If no leaf is spotted, YLS = N+1. A falling YLS means the spray programme is failing, because lesions develop faster than new leaves emerge. ProMusa describes a monitoring plot of 10 plants followed from establishment to flowering and replaced before flowering. — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)
- **Biological forecasting** is suited to low-disease-pressure areas without observed fungicide resistance. It times systemic sprays using climate-driven criteria based on fungus and plant development. — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)
- **Preaviso biológico / estado de evolución:** early symptoms are read on the youngest leaves (2, 3 and 4 in one source; 3, 4 and 5 in another). An Ecuadorian organic-banana study sampled 30 young plants weekly for a year. October had the highest evolution state, and September was the best month for phytosanitary action. It is used together with Stover severity scoring. — [UTMACH thesis 2020](https://repositorio.utmachala.edu.ec/bitstream/48000/16147/1/TTUACA-2020-IA-DE00030.pdf) (snippet); [ESPOL chapter](https://dspace.espol.edu.ec/bitstream/123456789/14729/6/CAPITULO%202.pdf) (snippet)
- Disease timing: chlorotic specks appear 14–20 days after infection of the unfurling "cigar" leaf. — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)

**Who monitors in Mexico**
- **SENASICA Foc R4T surveillance (2025):** drone exploration began February 2025 over **4,447 ha**. Satellite monitoring began July 2025 over **4,400 ha**. **667,348 plants** in producing regions were checked by laboratory tests during 2025. Explored area rose up to 800% compared with previous years. More than 16,000 risk products were intercepted at entry points, and inspection is being reinforced at 97 ports, airports and border crossings. — [Portal Frutícola, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=609837)
- SENASICA, through the DGSV and the national phytosanitary reference centre (CNRF), runs **Vigilancia Epidemiológica Fitosanitaria for Foc R4T in 16 states**. Its Feb 2026 notice is addressed to farmers, producer associations, "técnicos del Sistema Producto Plátano" and the "Organismos Auxiliares de Sanidad Vegetal" (the state plant-health committees). — [SENASICA Aviso Epidemiológico Foc R4T, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf)
- **Campaña contra Moko del plátano (federal):** in 2020–2021 it operated in Chiapas, Michoacán, Nayarit and Tabasco. Through Dec 2020 it served 1,140 production sites and 24,657 ha; through Apr 2021, 24,109 ha in 1,031 sites. — [SENASICA monthly report, Jan 2021](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/683622/1_INFORME_MENSUAL_ENERO_2021.pdf); [Apr 2021](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/683623/4_INFORME_MENSUAL_ABRIL_2021_2.pdf) (snippets, not opened)
- **Monitoring coverage among producers (2022, all crops):** 41.2% of production units in Tabasco and 34.9% in Chiapas did any pest/disease monitoring. — [INEGI CA 2022 Tabasco](https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/saladeprensa/boletines/2023/CA_Def/CA_Def2022_Tab.pdf); [INEGI CA 2022 Chiapas](https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/saladeprensa/boletines/2023/CA_Def/CA_Def2022_Chis.pdf)
- **Research and extension:** the CICY-led project (2018) developed methods for detecting Black Sigatoka and identified field reservoirs of the disease, which strengthened control practices. It was working on molecular detection of banana pathogens. — [Diario de Yucatán 2018](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)

**Cultural management**
- Remove diseased leaf tissue (deleafing) regularly. Beyond a certain level it can reduce bunch size. Nutrition, water management and plant density are part of integrated management. FHIA has bred resistant hybrids, but the export trade has adopted none. — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)
- In smallholder ("minifundista") plantings, cultural tasks such as deshije, deshoje, desmache, destronque and nutrition are often poorly done, which keeps pest and disease pressure high. A Colombian smallholder agreement had farmers commit to deshoje, despunte, deslamine, fertilization and weed management. — [Universidad Nacional de Colombia, via core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/427697708.pdf) (snippet, not opened); [Contexto Ganadero](https://www.contextoganadero.com/agricultura/la-sigatoka-negra-sera-atacada-en-varias-regiones-colombianas) (snippet, not opened)

### Inferences
- Today's methods need trained people walking the field every week and reading individual leaves. This is exactly the step that low-resource producers skip (59–65% do no monitoring). A drone-plus-AI map that tells a community where to send its people to deleaf or confirm disease replaces part of that labour, but not all of it.
- SENASICA's own adoption of drones (4,447 ha in 2025) and satellites (4,400 ha) for banana disease surveillance validates the team's approach in the eyes of Mexican authorities. Pitch the system as complementary: community-level, Sigatoka-focused, offline. SENASICA's effort is national and focused on Foc R4T.
- Because there is no evident Sigatoka-specific public campaign, smallholders mostly lack institutional monitoring for Sigatoka. That is the gap the project can fill. Plant-health committees (Organismos Auxiliares) and Sistema Producto technicians are natural operators or validators.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether any Comité Estatal de Sanidad Vegetal (CESVETAB, CESAVECHIS, CESAVECOL, etc.) currently runs Black Sigatoka monitoring or a preaviso network, or whether Sigatoka has an active SENASICA campaign after the 1987 quarantine. Ask the committees or check the SENASICA campaign list.
- No data on the cost or availability of private Sigatoka-monitoring technicians in Mexico.
- No Mexico-specific description of the "estado de evolución" protocol by INIFAP. The sources above are Ecuadorian.

## 4. Other threats relevant to the "other condition" class

### Takeaway
As of February 2026, SENASICA classifies Fusarium wilt TR4 (Foc R4T) as **absent from Mexico** but present in Colombia, Peru, Venezuela and Ecuador. Mexico runs surveillance in 16 states and a hotline for suspected cases. Moko (Ralstonia race 2) is present in Chiapas and Tabasco under a federal campaign. Both are visually confusable at leaf level with yellowing and wilting from other causes. An image classifier can only flag "suspicious, report it", never diagnose. Official diagnosis requires CNRF laboratory tests including PCR.

### Cited Findings
**Foc R4T**
- **Status:** under ISPM/NIMF No. 8, Foc R4T "ES UNA PLAGA AUSENTE EN MÉXICO: no hay registros de la plaga (IPPC, 2022)". Banana imports are prohibited under NOM-010-FITO-1995. — [SENASICA Aviso Epidemiológico, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf)
- **Americas:** present in Colombia, Peru, Venezuela and Ecuador. It was first reported in Taiwan in 1989. It survives in soil "hasta por 30 años", and contaminated soil can no longer grow bananas. No eradication-level control exists (Ploetz 2015; OIRSA 2025). — [SENASICA Aviso, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf)
- Peru, Oct 2025: Foc R4T affects banana and plantain in the Chira valley and Alto Piura (Piura). — [SENASICA Monitor Fitosanitario 16-10-2025](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/Contenido/files/2025/octubre/MonitorFitosanitario16102025_9af3b6a2-c081-4d72-81da-2dd3ce53f6c8.pdf) (snippet, not opened)
- **External symptoms:** older leaves yellow along the leaf margin toward the midrib, then weaken and collapse, hanging dry and brown around the pseudostem like a "falda" (skirt). **Internal symptoms:** yellow to dark-brown discoloration of the vascular bundles in roots, corm and pseudostem. Hosts are Cavendish (AAA) and cooking plantains (AAB), plus Heliconia and weeds such as Commelina diffusa, Chloris spp., Panicum purpurascens and Eleusine indica. — [SENASICA Aviso, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf)
- **Diagnosis and reporting:** the CNRF confirms by isolation, morphology and endpoint PCR (FocTR4-F/FocTR4-Rc primers). Anyone outside the surveillance programme who finds suspicious symptoms should report to DGSV at **(800) 98-79-879** or **alerta.fitosanitaria@senasica.gob.mx**. — [SENASICA Aviso, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf)
- SENASICA says the Moko campaign "coadyuva" (helps) R4T preparedness: field diagnosis, biosecurity and producer training. — [SENASICA Aviso, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf)

**Moko (Ralstonia solanacearum race 2)**
- Present in Chiapas and Tabasco, which are under phytosanitary control. The campaign also operates in Michoacán and Nayarit, and in 2020 covered 24,657 ha and 1,140 sites. In 2018 the Nayarit committee (CESAVENAY) reported 2,844.60 ha free of the disease, with the goal of a free-zone declaration. — [SENASICA informe enero 2021](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/683622/1_INFORME_MENSUAL_ENERO_2021.pdf) (snippet); [CESAVENAY IFF Oct 2018](https://cesavenay.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/Informes-Fisicos-Financieros/2018/10-IFF-OCTUBRE-MOKO.pdf) (snippet)

**Yellow Sigatoka (P. musae)**
- In Brazil, leaf wetness and mean temperature are its main weather drivers, and a severity-prediction model exists. — [Epagri, Revista Agropecuária Catarinense](https://publicacoes.epagri.sc.gov.br/rac/article/download/1862/1773/15884) (snippet, not opened)

### Inferences
- The "other condition" class should be framed as **"anomaly, needs human or official verification"**. Do not label it Fusarium or Moko. Foc R4T yellowing of older leaves from the margin, and the collapsed-leaf skirt, could be seen from a drone, but confirmation needs a pseudostem cut and CNRF PCR. One concrete public-good feature: the app could carry the SENASICA hotline and email and produce a georeferenced report for any plant flagged as wilting or yellowing.
- Foc R4T is in Colombia, Peru, Venezuela and Ecuador, and SENASICA is scaling drone and satellite surveillance. Early community-level anomaly flags would therefore be relevant to national biosecurity, which is a strong impact argument for partners.

### Gaps
- No sources found on nutrient deficiencies in Mexican banana that look like Sigatoka (K, Mg or N chlorosis, for example), or on banana bunchy top / banana streak virus status in Mexico.
- No current (2024–2026) Moko campaign coverage figures; the latest found were 2020–2021.

## 5. Climate drivers of Sigatoka and forecasting / early-warning models

### Takeaway
Black Sigatoka pressure follows rain, humidity and leaf wetness, and more sprays are needed in the rainy season. A 59-year climate analysis for Teapa, Tabasco finds October and March the most favourable months, with autumn and winter more favourable than spring and summer. Predictive models exist (Costa Rica, global CLIMEX), but I found **no operational Black Sigatoka early-warning system in Mexico**.

### Cited Findings
- **Teapa, Tabasco:** temperature data for 1961–2019 from the Teapa station. "October and March are the most favorable months for Black Sigatoka occurrence". Spring and summer are least favourable, fall and winter more favourable. No "highly favorable" conditions were found overall. Authors: Aceves Navarro (Colegio de Postgraduados), Rivera-Hernández (Universidad Popular de la Chontalpa), Arrieta-Rivera (TecNM IT Zona Olmeca), Juárez-López (Colegio de Postgraduados, Campus Tabasco); published 2024. They recommend timing integrated management for the favourable period and repeating the study in other Tabasco zones. — [Revista Agroproductividad, art. 2500](https://revista-agroproductividad.org/index.php/agroproductividad/article/view/2500)
- **Costa Rica (2022):** models for early prediction of leaf infection (percent necrotic leaf area). The climate variables most related to infection were precipitation, maximum and minimum temperature, relative humidity, leaf wetness and solar radiation. — [Revista Agroinnovación / TEC Costa Rica](https://revistas.tec.ac.cr/index.php/agroinn/issue/download/646/160) (snippet, not opened)
- **Global CLIMEX niche model:** a moderately strong non-linear relationship between modelled climate suitability for P. fijiensis and expert disease-pressure ratings. Useful for mapping risk regions rather than forecasting outbreaks. — ["Black Sigatoka in bananas: Ecoclimatic suitability and disease pressure assessments"](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/black-sigatoka-in-bananas-ecoclimatic-suitability-and-disease-pressure-assessments/812743950006747137-10889) (snippet, not opened)
- Rainy season: a Mexican grower sprays every 8 days, more often in the rains. — [Portal Frutícola](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=20910) (snippet, not opened)
- Biological forecasting can space out sprays in low-pressure regions without resistance. — [ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)
- Ecuadorian preaviso study: October had the highest evolution state, and September was the best month to act. — [UTMACH 2020](https://repositorio.utmachala.edu.ec/bitstream/48000/16147/1/TTUACA-2020-IA-DE00030.pdf) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Without changing hardware, the app could combine detection maps with a simple seasonal risk calendar (for Tabasco, October and March peaks) to schedule drone flights, for example more often in rainy and autumn months. That turns one-off detection into a low-cost early-warning routine. Any risk rule must be presented as indicative, since only the Teapa study is Mexican.

### Gaps
- No quantitative thresholds (optimal temperature, RH %, leaf-wetness hours) from a source I could open. A search for this hit a tool error and I did not repeat it. Classic literature values (e.g., Jacome & Schuh; Marín et al. 2003 in Plant Disease) should be checked directly.
- No Mexican operational early-warning service (from SENASICA, INIFAP or state committees) was found.

## 6. Potential partners or users in Mexico and comparable Latin America

### Takeaway
The most credible partners are the institutions already working on Black Sigatoka and banana surveillance in southeast Mexico. These include CICY (Yucatán) and its research network (INIFAP, UJAT, UACh, U. de Colima, UAG), Colegio de Postgraduados–Campus Tabasco, SENASICA/DGSV/CNRF and the state plant-health committees, Sistema Producto Plátano technicians, and grower associations such as the Asociación de Plataneros del Soconusco and Fundación Produce Tabasco.

### Cited Findings
- **CICY, Unidad de Biotecnología** (lead: Dra. Blondy Canto Canché), with Universidad Autónoma Chapingo, Universidad Juárez Autónoma de Tabasco, INIFAP, Universidad de Colima and Universidad Autónoma de Guadalajara: Sigatoka detection methods and 20–40% fungicide reduction in Tabasco and Colima; field work also in Chiapas and Oaxaca (2018). — [Diario de Yucatán 2018](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)
- **Colegio de Postgraduados (Campus Tabasco), Universidad Popular de la Chontalpa, TecNM IT Zona Olmeca:** authors of the 2024 Teapa Sigatoka climate study. — [Agroproductividad 2024](https://revista-agroproductividad.org/index.php/agroproductividad/article/view/2500)
- **SENASICA (DGSV, CNRF, Programa de Vigilancia Epidemiológica Fitosanitaria), Sistema Producto Plátano technicians, Organismos Auxiliares de Sanidad Vegetal:** named recipients of the 2026 Foc R4T notice; they run drone and satellite surveillance. — [SENASICA Aviso, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf); [Portal Frutícola Jan 2026](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=609837)
- **CESAVENAY** (Nayarit plant-health committee): runs the Moko campaign operations in Nayarit. — [CESAVENAY reports](https://cesavenay.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/Informes-Fisicos-Financieros/2018/10-IFF-OCTUBRE-MOKO.pdf) (snippet)
- **Fundación Produce Tabasco:** its president was the source of the 2013 Tabasco control-cost figures. — [FreshPlaza 2013](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/2110011/mexico-banana-producers-lose-100-million-dollars-a-year/)
- **Asociación de Plataneros del Soconusco** (Chiapas): an organized grower group exporting via Puerto Chiapas. — [Portal Frutícola](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=12136) (snippet, not opened)
- **ECOSUR** (El Colegio de la Frontera Sur) library holds Mexican fungicide-sensitivity work on M. fijiensis. — [ECOSUR biblioteca](https://biblioteca.ecosur.mx/bib/52390)
- **OIRSA** is cited by SENASICA (2025) on Foc R4T control. It is a regional plant-health body covering Mexico, Central America and the Dominican Republic. — [SENASICA Aviso, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The best pilot users are probably organized smallholder groups (an ejido, a cooperative, or Asociación de Plataneros members) in Teapa, Tacotalpa or Cunduacán (Tabasco), or in Soconusco and the Chiapas border region. The border region reported up to 40% Sigatoka losses in 2021. Plant-health committee technicians could validate detections and use the maps to target extension.
- Linking "other condition" flags to the SENASICA reporting channel makes the system useful to the national Foc R4T effort. That gives authorities a reason to support community deployment.

### Gaps
- No World Banana Forum, Bioversity/Alliance or FAO smallholder Sigatoka programme active in Mexico was confirmed in this research.
- No specific banana cooperatives, ejidos or indigenous banana-growing communities were identified by name in Tabasco or Chiapas. Indigenous areas near Tabasco banana zones (for example the Chontal and Ch'ol regions) are a lead to check with the INPI and the INEGI CA 2022 municipal tabulados.
- No named NGO working specifically with plantain smallholders on Sigatoka in Mexico was found.
