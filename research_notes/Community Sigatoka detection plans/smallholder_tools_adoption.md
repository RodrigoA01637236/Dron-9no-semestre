# Deployed AI Plant-Disease Diagnosis Tools for Low-Resource Smallholders: Adoption, Impact, and Lessons (2016–2026)

Scope note: These notes compare real deployments (PlantVillage Nuru, Tumaini, Plantix, ICT4BXW, FAO FAMEWS, Farmer.Chat, Latin American Black Sigatoka tools) and what made them work or fail with poor, rural, low-literacy, low-connectivity users. The goal is to inform the redesign of a Tec de Monterrey drone + YOLOv8n-cls + offline Flask "ground station" for Black Sigatoka detection with smallholders, cooperatives and indigenous communities in Chiapas/Tabasco. Evidence quality is flagged throughout: peer-reviewed field evaluation > project reports and newsletters > press and vendor claims.

---

## 1. Which tools exist, what do they do, and which run offline on cheap phones?

### Takeaway
The best-documented deployed tools are PlantVillage Nuru (cassava and others; on-device model, 23 languages), Tumaini (banana; free Android, later Android/iOS; 5 diseases + 1 pest), Plantix (commercial, 20M+ downloads, mostly India; weak offline support), and ICT4BXW (banana wilt; app plus SMS/IVR/USSD for basic phones). For Black Sigatoka specifically there are only niche or research tools (Sigatocare by ITK/CIRAD, SIGAPLANT, Colombian and Ecuadorian research apps, a 2025 smartphone IoT prototype). I found no AI Sigatoka diagnosis tool deployed with smallholders in Chiapas or Tabasco.

### Cited Findings

**PlantVillage Nuru (Penn State / IITA / CGIAR / FAO partners)**
- Nuru was developed as a smartphone object-detection model. The original cassava model was trained on 2,415 cassava leaf images and tested in a mobile app on 720 diseased leaflets in a field in Tanzania. Citation: Ramcharan A., McCloskey P., Baranowski K., Mbilinyi N., Mrisho L., Ndalahwa M., Legg J., Hughes D.P. - Nuru diagnoses the foliar symptoms of cassava mosaic disease (CMD) and cassava brown streak disease (CBSD). Citation: Mrisho L.M. et al. (2020), "Accuracy of a Smartphone-Based Object Detection Model, PlantVillage Nuru, in Identifying the Foliar Symptoms of the Viral Diseases of Cassava–CMD and CBSD," *Frontiers in Plant Science* 11:590889, DOI 10.3389/fpls.2020.590889 — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.590889/full); [PMC7775399](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7775399/)
- In the Côte d'Ivoire deployment the app offered 23 languages, but basic literacy was still needed to use it. The paper also says internet access was needed to upload images. Citation: Adjéi E.A., Traoré K., Sawadogo/Compaoré E.M.F.W., Amoakon W.J.-L., Kouassi N.K., Kouassi M.K., Pita J.S. - One Kenyan news report says farmers asked for an offline version of "PlantVillage+" and for local-language options. This is press coverage, not a study. — [iAfrica.com](https://iafrica.com/kenyan-smallholder-farmers-use-ai-apps-to-detect-crop-disease-and-cut-costs-as-agritech-spreads-across-africa/)

**Tumaini (Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT; banana)**
- Tumaini is a smartphone app built on CNNs and transfer learning. It identifies five major banana diseases and one pest from photos of any plant part (fruit, bunch or leaves). It was tested in Colombia, DR Congo, India, Benin, China and Uganda, and it is free on Android. Citation: Selvaraj M.G. et al. - A CGIAR MEL project record reports "over 3000 downloads" (an early-stage figure). — [CGIAR MEL](https://mel.cgiar.org/projects/-15/347/farmers-can-more-easily-assess-the-health-status-of-their-banana-fields-by-using-an-ai-powered-smartphone-app)
- The latest Tumaini version for Android and iOS includes ground-level disease detection models. It is built on a georeferenced banana database of more than 70,000 banana plants collected between 2015 and 2024. Citation: Mora, Blomme et al. (2025), "Digital framework for georeferenced multiplatform surveillance of banana wilt using human in the loop AI and YOLO foundation models," *Scientific Reports*, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-87588-2 (open access, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0) — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-87588-2); [Alliance record](https://alliancebioversityciat.org/publications-data/digital-framework-georeferenced-multiplatform-surveillance-banana-wilt-using); [CGSpace](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/items/27c5e2de-e6d9-48b1-b934-d46338c88959)
- In the Rwanda deployment Tumaini was available in French, Swahili and English only. It had no Kinyarwanda, the language every participant spoke. Citation: Ndayizeye J.P., Omondi B.A., Kawarazuka N., Bigirimana V.d.P., Gaidashova S. - **Agrio** has a Spanish-language Black Sigatoka library page. Its detection accuracy and offline behaviour were not evaluated here. — [Agrio](https://agrio.app/library/BlackSigatoka-12/)

### Inferences
- No existing tool covers the team's niche: offline, Spanish- and indigenous-language, cooperative-level, drone + phone, Black Sigatoka-specific, for southern Mexico. This is a real gap, not duplication. Tumaini (banana, multi-disease) and Sigatocare (Sigatoka spray-timing indicator) are the closest references to study and possibly partner with.
- "Offline" in this field usually means on-device inference. Registration, installation, updates and image upload still need connectivity, and the Côte d'Ivoire study shows those steps are where things break. The team's local Flask ground station avoids several of these failure points by design.

### Gaps
- I could not verify from primary text whether current Nuru, Plantix and Agrio builds run full diagnosis with no connectivity, or what minimum Android specs they need.
- I found no deployed AI Sigatoka tool documented with Mexican smallholders or indigenous communities. SENASICA, ECOSUR, INIFAP and the state Comités Estatales de Sanidad Vegetal were not searched.
- I did not research WhatsApp-native image-diagnosis bots beyond Farmer.Chat, for example Kenyan or Indian WhatsApp crop bots.

## 2. What evidence exists on adoption, field accuracy, user trust, and measurable impact?

### Takeaway
Field accuracy is well below lab accuracy. Nuru scored about 65% on single leaves and 74–88% when six leaves per plant were assessed, and a model's real-world F1 fell 32%. Even so, apps can beat untrained extension agents (40–58%) and farmers (18–31%). Adoption drops sharply after projects end: in Côte d'Ivoire about 45% kept using the app, and in Rwanda 39% of trained farmers used it, with 26% of women vs 61% of men. Access (owning a smartphone, data, language) matters more than ability. There is no rigorous evidence that AI diagnosis apps change yield, pesticide use or income. The best causal evidence is for digital agricultural advice in general: about +4% yield and +22% odds of adoption (Fabregas et al. 2019).

### Cited Findings

**Field accuracy**
- **Nuru vs humans (Tanzania/Kenya cassava):** Nuru reached 65% accuracy (2020 model), against 40–58% for agricultural extension agents and 18–31% for farmers. — [Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.590889/full)
- **Multiple leaves per plant:** Assessing six leaves per plant raised Nuru's in-field accuracy to 74–88%. — [Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7775399/)
- **Model version and phone:** The 2018 model (V1.05) scored only 30–41% on three of the phones tested. The 2020 model (V2.6) scored 62–68% across all four phones. — [Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.590889/full)
- **Lab-to-field drop:** The F1 score fell 32% for pronounced symptoms in real-world images, the data closest to the training set, because recall dropped. The authors say recall must be tuned for real-world use, and they found performance differed between image and video input. — [Ramcharan et al. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2019.00272/pdf)
- **Image capture errors:** Improper framing caused about 30% of field misdiagnoses in one evaluation (Mrisho et al., 2020). Low-quality smartphone images reduced accuracy by up to 40% (Athindehou 2023). Both figures are cited secondarily in the Rwanda paper. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Tumaini accuracy, conflicting figures:** The press reported about 90% for the beta version. When Tech Briefs asked about the missed 10%, the lead researcher said the app was still in testing. — [Tech Briefs](https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/35024-ai-and-agriculture-how-to-spot-bad-bananas-with-a-phone); [Inceptive Mind](https://www.inceptivemind.com/ai-tool-tumaini-banana-diseases-pests-90-accuracy/8499/). The Alliance itself cites 70–99% for regional models, depending on plant part and disease (cited in [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)).
- **Banana wilt multiplatform system (Sci Rep 2025):** On drone imagery YOLOv9 performed best, with mAP@50, precision and recall of 55–86% for healthy plants, Fusarium wilt and Xanthomonas wilt. On ground images YOLOv8 reached 65–99% depending on plant part and region. — [Alliance record](https://alliancebioversityciat.org/publications-data/digital-framework-georeferenced-multiplatform-surveillance-banana-wilt-using)

**Adoption and continued use**
- **Côte d'Ivoire Nuru (146 farmers, surveyed August 2023):** About 45% kept using Nuru for CMD after the project ended and 56% stopped (the paper's figures). Continued use varied by department: Dabou 61%, Bouaké 54%, Man 23%. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **What farmers used Nuru for:** monitoring field health (98%), selecting healthy cuttings (52%) and getting advice (21%). — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Why use declined:** Use fell as extension support ended. Farmers also said they had learned to recognize CMD on their own, so they needed the app less. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Determinants of continued use (marginal effects):** family labor +34%, cultivated area +25%, participation in awareness campaigns +18%, monitoring by an extension officer +17%, a smartphone provided by the WAVE project +15%, owning a smartphone +15%, male gender +13%, internet access +12%, education +11%. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Rwanda Tumaini sample:** 357 surveyed farmers, 61 trained farmers interviewed, and 40 smartphone owners monitored through WhatsApp for 12 months. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Rwanda willingness vs use:** 85.4% said they were willing to use the app before training, but only 39.3% of trained farmers (24/61) used it afterwards: 26.3% of women vs 60.9% of men (p = 0.024). — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Rwanda independent use:** 47.8% of men vs 26.3% of women used the app on their own. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Rwanda engagement over 12 months:** mean active days were 34.8 for women vs 56.0 for men, and mean diagnosis results per farmer were 6.9 vs 9.5. Per active day, engagement was similar, so the gap is in access, not in ability. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Rwanda knowledge sharing:** 47.4% of women and 56.5% of men shared Tumaini with neighbours. Sharing reached 337 community members through women and 754 through men. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **ICT4BXW reach (self-reported, 2023):** 89,707 callers and 82,807 listeners on the 845 USSD/IVR line, and 58,572 farmers received early-warning SMS alerts. The BXW app counted 3,041 farmers and 4,347 diagnosed farms. — [ICT4BXW Newsletter III](https://www.iita.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ICT4BXW-Newsletter-III-Edition.pdf). An earlier IITA blog cites 8,000 diagnosed farms, which conflicts. — [IITA blog](https://blogs.iita.org/index.php/rwandan-farmers-and-extension-agents-commend-iitas-digital-tools-for-sustainable-banana-production/)
- **ICT4BXW pilot and end-line:** Phase I piloted with about 2,500 farmers and farmer promoters across 8 districts. A 2021 end-line survey covered 233 farmers and 61 farmer promoters. — [Rural21 2021](https://www2.rural21.com/fileadmin/downloads/2021/en-01/rural2021_01-S23-25.pdf)
- **Plantix pilot (urban vegetable farmers, Hanoi):** Women and men were both greatly interested, and some farmers bought the pesticide the app recommended. The pilot was small and ran off-season. — [CGSpace pilot report](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/bitstreams/e8e51f60-d487-49d3-828b-63da386b4866/download)

**Trust**
- **Côte d'Ivoire:** 93% said Nuru consistently recognized CMD, and 54% were very satisfied while 46% were satisfied. The abstract claims "100%" considered it essential and reliable, but the body's breakdown of reliability ratings is internally confusing (59%/41%). Treat the 100% figure cautiously. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Kenya:** A researcher quoted in the press says trust depends on transparency about how farmer data is collected, stored and used. — [iAfrica.com](https://iafrica.com/kenyan-smallholder-farmers-use-ai-apps-to-detect-crop-disease-and-cut-costs-as-agritech-spreads-across-africa/)

**Measurable impact (yield, pesticide, income)**
- **Fabregas, Kremer and Schilbach (2019), "Realizing the potential of digital development: The case of agricultural advice," *Science*, DOI 10.1126/science.aay3038.** This meta-analysis of RCTs in sub-Saharan Africa and India found that mobile-delivered agricultural information raised yields by about 4%, and that phone promotion of a recommended input raised the odds of adoption by about 22% across six studies. Two studies reported short-term benefit-cost ratios of 5:1 to 10:1, and effects vary by year (for example, in droughts). This covers general advisory, not AI diagnosis. — [Precision Development summary](https://precisiondev.org/?p=756); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/618124)
- **Farmer.Chat, self-reported:** 60–80% of active users said they changed practices within 30–60 days. Cost-per-farmer claims vary across Digital Green sources, from $35 down to $0.35 or $3.50. — [AgDaily](https://www.agdaily.com/technology/this-ai-chatbot-delivers-farm-advice-in-10-plus-languages/)
- **Farmer.Chat RCT:** A randomized trial in Kenya with IFPRI is registered. It measures knowledge, adoption, productivity and income over two seasons; results were not found. — [AEA RCT Registry #17035](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/17035)
- **Côte d'Ivoire authors:** They explicitly call for comparing beneficiary and non-beneficiary areas to measure effects on CMD incidence, yield and income. This implies no such impact data exists for Nuru there. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Plantix:** Claims of pesticide reduction come from FAO/vendor profiles. No controlled evaluation was found. — [FAO STI portal](https://sti-portal.fao.org/innovations/plantix)

### Inferences
- A single-image classifier gives an optimistic picture. Field accuracy improves a lot when results are aggregated across several leaves or plants. For the team this argues for reporting a plant- or plot-level verdict that combines many drone frames and phone photos, rather than one photo's top-1 label.
- Accuracy varies by phone and model version (30–41% vs 62–68%). The team should test YOLOv8n-cls on the actual cheap phones and cameras used in Chiapas and Tabasco, at low resolution, in sun and shade, before claiming any number.
- The strongest adoption predictors are human and social: extension monitoring, awareness campaigns and device access. Extension monitoring (+17%) and awareness campaigns (+18%) had about the same effect as owning a smartphone (+15%). Software design alone will not drive adoption.
- Because measurable impact evidence is missing, a small, well-designed before/after or comparison-plot evaluation (Sigatoka severity, fungicide sprays, yield) would be an original contribution.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed RCT or quasi-experiment linking any AI plant-disease diagnosis app to yield, pesticide use or income.
- No independent per-disease accuracy breakdown for Tumaini in the field was found. The Selvaraj 2019 paper reports test-set results, which I did not read in full here.
- No Black Sigatoka-specific field accuracy for smartphone or drone tools under smallholder conditions was retrieved.

## 3. What design patterns work for low-resource users?

### Takeaway
The patterns with the best support are: offline-first with store-and-forward; voice plus local language plus pictograms; low-friction onboarding; delivery that does not require a smartphone (SMS/IVR/USSD); mediation by extension agents or farmer promoters with sustained follow-up; group and shared-device access; capture guidance and multi-image sampling; and pairing each diagnosis with a concrete, locally feasible action. Evidence comes mostly from deployment surveys (Rwanda, Côte d'Ivoire, ICT4BXW), not from controlled UX trials.

### Cited Findings
- **Language is a hard barrier.** Only 12.2% of men and 6.8% of women in Rwanda could read any of Tumaini's three app languages, even though Kinyarwanda literacy was 86.2% and 71.8%. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **What trained Rwandan farmers asked for:** more training (79%), Kinyarwanda localization (34%) and device provision (15%). — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Rwanda authors' design recommendations:** localize into Kinyarwanda and Kirundi; add voice prompts, pictograms and visual support; provide structured group-based, in-person support; involve farmers early in design; test on low-resolution images and a range of devices; and work on device access through women's groups. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Data cost shapes use patterns.** Most Rwandan activity happened in the evening, when farmers had time and had bought data bundles. Men's main barriers were internet costs and installation problems. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Onboarding friction.** In Côte d'Ivoire, 41% found the app complex, specifically registration and required field entries. 33% had installation failures, mostly from poor internet, and 6% hit technical problems such as phone-brand incompatibility and missed updates. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Basic-phone channels scale.** ICT4BXW added IVR, SMS and USSD because up to 60% of Rwandan farmers use basic phones, and those channels reached tens of thousands (89,707 IVR/USSD callers; 58,572 SMS alert recipients). — [ICT4BXW Newsletter III](https://www.iita.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ICT4BXW-Newsletter-III-Edition.pdf)
- **IVR for intermediaries.** ICT4BXW used IVR remote training aimed at more than 1,000 farmer promoters. — [ICT4BXW Newsletter III](https://www.iita.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ICT4BXW-Newsletter-III-Edition.pdf)
- **Women's phones.** Women more often owned keypad phones (65–70% vs 50–62% for men), while total phone ownership was similar (71–84%). A smartphone-only tool therefore excludes women disproportionately. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Mediated use is the norm.** Many Rwandan women relied on children or others to navigate the app. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Extension mediation sustains use.** Monitoring by an extension officer (+17%) and awareness campaigns (+18%) raised continued use. The authors recommend prolonged follow-up and a continuous exchange platform between users and the project team. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Pair diagnosis with action.** The Côte d'Ivoire authors stress that the app only aids identification and must be paired with healthy planting material and CMD-resistant varieties. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Spray-timing output for Sigatoka.** Sigatocare turns Black Sigatoka observations into a color-coded indicator of whether fungicide applications are needed in the coming days. — [ITK](https://www.itk.fr/en/news/black-sigatoka-disease-of-banana-symptoms-control-and-indicators/)
- **Sampling and capture protocol.** Assessing six leaves per plant (instead of one) raised accuracy to 74–88%. — [Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7775399/). Poor framing caused about 30% of misdiagnoses (cited in [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)).
- **Voice and offline for non-literate users** (project self-reports): the Ghana app's Twi text-to-voice — [AI4D Ghana](https://www.ai4d.ai/innovations/digital-literacy-and-ai-for-african-crop-disease-detection-in-ghana); the Nigerian RiceAdvisor recommendation for offline-first design — [IJAEE](https://www.acspublisher.com/journals/index.php/ijaee/article/download/23329/20208/42077)
- **Grounded advice for extension agents.** Farmer.Chat restricts its answers to vetted local extension content and serves extension agents (4,500+) as well as farmers. — [Gooey.AI](https://gooey.ai/farmer-chat); [ITU DASF slides](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-D/Regional-Presence/AsiaPacific/SiteAssets/Pages/Events/2023/Digital%20Agriculture%20Solutions%20Forum%20(DASF)/Digital-Agriculture-Solutions-Forum/S2_P1_Pallassana_Digital%20Green.pdf)
- **Characterize users first.** The Colombian Black Sigatoka system characterized its agricultural users before building the classifier. — [Escudero et al. 2021, AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/zh/records/675acdd00ce2cede71d1a18a)

### Inferences (design implications for the team's ground station and model)
- **Offline-first and cooperative-held.** Keep the Flask ground station fully offline, with on-device or LAN inference. Sync with store-and-forward when connectivity appears. Ship it as a shared community or cooperative device (a laptop or Raspberry Pi with a Wi-Fi hotspot, so phones connect through a browser and no app install is needed). This avoids the install and registration failures seen in Côte d'Ivoire (33% install failures, 41% found it complex) and the gaps in smartphone ownership.
- **Zero-friction entry.** No accounts are needed to diagnose. Ask only for an optional plot or community tag. Make photo-first or drone-batch-first the primary flow.
- **Language and literacy.** Use a Spanish interface with large icons, traffic-light severity colours and audio playback of results and advice. Plan for recorded audio in the indigenous languages actually spoken by the target communities. In Chiapas these likely include Tseltal, Tsotsil and Ch'ol, but the team must confirm with the communities; these language names are not sourced in these notes.
- **Reach basic phones.** Use optional SMS or voice alerts from the station to basic phones for plot-level warnings, following the ICT4BXW model. A WhatsApp export of the results map and summary could reach smartphone users.
- **Capture guidance and aggregation.** Give on-screen framing guides (a leaf outline, distance hints) and require several leaves per plant. For example, follow the standard Sigatoka practice of the youngest leaves, mirroring Nuru's six-leaf finding. Aggregate drone frames into a per-plant or per-row verdict.
- **Actionable outputs.** Every result should end in a concrete next step that is feasible locally: remove or prune infected leaves (deleaf), check drainage, or contact a technician. For larger growers it could give a spray-need indicator like Sigatocare's. Avoid naming commercial products.
- **Design for "learning, then less need."** Côte d'Ivoire farmers learned to recognize CMD themselves. Make the tool teach symptom stages with images of Sigatoka stages 1–6, and treat lower usage over time as possible success, not only as abandonment.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled comparison of UX variants (icons vs text, voice vs none) for plant-disease apps.
- There is no direct evidence on indigenous-language interfaces for plant-disease AI in Latin America.
- WhatsApp-native diagnosis bots and their adoption metrics were not covered in the sources retrieved.

## 4. What failure modes and ethical issues are documented?

### Takeaway
Documented failure modes are: the lab-to-field accuracy collapse (especially in recall); bad photo framing and out-of-distribution inputs; phone and model-version variability; dependence on connectivity for installing and uploading; abandonment once project support ends; and exclusion of women and the least-resourced. Ethical concerns centre on commercial capture (Plantix's documented move into agrochemical sales), unclear data ownership, farm data used without consent, and the weak bargaining power of smallholders.

### Cited Findings
- **Recall loss in the field.** F1 dropped 32% in real-world images because recall dropped, meaning diseases were missed. — [Ramcharan et al. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2019.00272/pdf)
- **Out-of-distribution inputs.** Wadhwani AI researchers (White, Madaan, Shenoy, Agnihotri, Sharma, Doshi; NeurIPS 2022 workshop) argue that existing sample-rejection methods are ill-suited to resource-limited, early-stage ML deployments, where robust and diverse training data is hard to get. They propose a simple rejection baseline. — [arXiv 2208.06359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06359)
- **Device and version dependence.** The older Nuru model scored 30–41% on three phones. — [Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.590889/full)
- **Connectivity and installation.** 33% had installation failures from poor internet, 11% lost their smartphone or had it withdrawn by the project, and 65% lacked a personal smartphone. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Post-project abandonment.** Use fell as extension support ended, and continued use was only 23% in Man. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Smartphone ownership gap (Rwanda).** 24% of men vs 6.5% of women owned smartphones (OR 4.47). Electricity access predicted ownership (OR 8.71); income and land size did not. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Internet access gap (Rwanda).** 20.3% of men vs 4.7% of women had internet access. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Household barriers.** Focus groups cited male headship norms and marital trust concerns over women's phone use. Women also reported device damage and theft. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Domain shift.** An app trained on images from one region may be less accurate for crop varieties or diseases elsewhere. This is a general overview claim, not a documented case. — [ResearchGate: Smartphone-Based Diagnostics for Smallholder Farmers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404663326_Smartphone-Based_Diagnostics_for_Smallholder_Farmers)
- **Commercial capture.** Plantix moved from its original pesticide-reduction mission to selling agrochemicals directly with an integrated retailer ecosystem. — [Agrisnip 2025](https://agrisnip.com/asafal/plantixs-pivot-from-pesticide-reduction-to-agrochemical-sales-and-a). The article's claim that venture-capital pressure drove this is the author's opinion. Another source says the app links users with local input sellers and takes a commission on app-generated pesticide sales. — [Wikipedia: Plantix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantix) (secondary; verify)
- **Advice that can drive purchases.** In the Hanoi Plantix pilot, some farmers bought the recommended pesticide. — [CGSpace pilot report](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/bitstreams/e8e51f60-d487-49d3-828b-63da386b4866/download)
- **Data value capture.** Farmers generate the most data but gain the least from it. Ownership of data collected through digital tools is uncertain, and legal protection for non-personal farm data is weak. — [AIMS/GODAN Farm Data MOOC, Lesson 2.2](https://aims.gitbook.io/farm-data-mooc/untitled/lesson-2.2-challenges-for-smallholders-in-data-value-chains)
- **Use without consent.** Farmer organizations and companies collect a lot of data, even in developing countries, and it is often used without permission or acknowledgement. — [GFAR/GODAN brief on farmers' data](https://www.gfar.net/includas-sub/content/brief-1-farmers-data-ethical-and-legal-considerations-about-data-sharing-aspects-smart); [GFAIR: Rights of farmers for data, information and knowledge](https://gfair.network/sites/default/files/rights_of_farmers_for_data_information_and_knowledge.pdf)
- **Weak bargaining power.** Smallholders have very little negotiating power, and trust is often missing in data relationships. — [Springer chapter (2022)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-13276-6_13)
- **Governance alternatives and pushback.** FarmStack (Digital Green) is a decentralized data-sharing protocol meant to let farmers keep control of their data. Indian farm organizations have pushed back on the AgriStack registry over privacy. — [Development Gateway case study (2023)](https://developmentgateway.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Ecosysten_CaseStudy_Feb2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- The team's "uncertainty-first human review" already addresses the overtrust and out-of-distribution risks. It should be strengthened with an explicit "No sé / foto no válida" (abstain or reject) output, so that out-of-distribution or low-quality images never get a confident Sigatoka/healthy label.
- For early Sigatoka detection, missing an infection is more costly than a false alarm. The model threshold should favour recall, following Ramcharan's recommendation, and false alarms should be caught by human review.
- Georeferenced photos of indigenous and smallholder plots are sensitive: they reveal location, disease status and possibly land tenure. The team should use community-level consent, keep data local by default, let the cooperative own and export its data, and not share with input sellers. Plantix's trajectory shows the risk of advice turning into sales.
- Because the station runs offline and on local hardware, it structurally reduces both connectivity dependence and extractive cloud data flows. This is worth stating as a design value.

### Gaps
- I found no documented case of a specific crop loss caused by a wrong AI plant diagnosis.
- The Wadhwani AI paper's specific deployment and its out-of-distribution or rejection numbers were not retrieved, because the PDF could not be parsed.
- No studies on data sovereignty specific to indigenous communities (for example, CARE principles applied to agricultural AI in Mexico) were retrieved.

## 5. How do human-in-the-loop, citizen-science and extension-worker validation models work in practice?

### Takeaway
In working deployments, AI is a triage or decision aid inside a human network. Trained intermediaries (extension officers, farmer promoters, national focal points) validate reports, and experts refine models. Using the app alone barely improves human skill in the short term: two weeks of Nuru use left extension workers' diagnostic scores essentially unchanged. Validation therefore needs trained reviewers, not just any user.

### Cited Findings
- **Nuru as an aid to extension workers.** Two weeks of practical use gave only a slight, not statistically significant, increase in extension workers' diagnostic skill (about 49.9% before vs 50.5% after). The authors suggest longer field experience is needed. — [Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.590889/full); [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.01.26.919449v2.full)
- **Human baseline.** Untrained human baselines are low: extension agents scored 40–58% and farmers 18–31%. — [Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7775399/)
- **Human-in-the-loop banana wilt surveillance (Sci Rep 2025).** Human-in-the-loop AI refines the ground-level model's predictions. Grad-CAM explainability adds transparency. Drone and ground platforms are combined over a georeferenced database of 70,000+ plants spanning Africa, Latin America, India, Asia and Australia. — [CGSpace](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/items/27c5e2de-e6d9-48b1-b934-d46338c88959); [Alliance record](https://alliancebioversityciat.org/publications-data/digital-framework-georeferenced-multiplatform-surveillance-banana-wilt-using)
- **ICT4BXW citizen science.** Farmer promoters were trained (more than 1,000 through IVR) and given government-provided smartphones. Farmers gave feedback on how useful the SMS alerts were. — [ICT4BXW Newsletter III](https://www.iita.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ICT4BXW-Newsletter-III-Edition.pdf); [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **FAMEWS tiered validation.** Farmer and agricultural-worker reports are validated by national FAW focal points before they enter the global platform and maps. — [IEEE GRSS](https://www.grss-ieee.org/events/fall-armyworm-monitoring-and-early-warning-system-famews/)
- **Extension-officer monitoring.** Being monitored by an extension officer raised the probability of continued Nuru use by 17%. — [Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)
- **Farmer.Chat's model.** It supports human extension agents with an AI assistant, with more than 4,500 extension agents as of January 2024, rather than replacing them. — [ITU DASF slides](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-D/Regional-Presence/AsiaPacific/SiteAssets/Pages/Events/2023/Digital%20Agriculture%20Solutions%20Forum%20(DASF)/Digital-Agriculture-Solutions-Forum/S2_P1_Pallassana_Digital%20Green.pdf)

### Inferences
- The team's design (uncertainty-first review queue, then batch retraining) matches the Sci Rep 2025 human-in-the-loop pattern closely. It should add role tiers: a farmer or community observer captures; a trained cooperative promoter or technician confirms; an expert (for example, a state plant-health technician or a university plant pathologist) resolves disagreements. Only expert-confirmed labels should enter retraining, so that noisy labels do not get in.
- Explainability views such as Grad-CAM heatmaps on the leaf in the review screen can help reviewers check the model and build calibrated trust. They also double as teaching material.
- Reviewers need structured training. Mere exposure to the app did not raise extension workers' skill.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data was found on review turnaround times, label-correction rates or reviewer agreement in Tumaini or Nuru human-in-the-loop pipelines.
- The details of Tumaini's expert-feedback workflow (who reviews, and how corrections flow back to farmers) were not retrievable from the Sci Rep abstract-level sources.

## 6. Are there community-level or cooperative-level disease surveillance programs (shared maps, early warning) using drones or phones?

### Takeaway
Yes, though they are mostly in Africa and mostly use phones: FAO FAMEWS (validated reports feeding global maps), ICT4BXW (citizen-science reports plus SMS early-warning alerts to more than 58,000 farmers) and the Alliance's 2025 drone + ground banana-wilt framework. The 2025 Sigatoka smartphone-IoT prototype maps georeferenced infection stages. I found no cooperative-run drone Sigatoka surveillance program for smallholders in Mexico.

### Cited Findings
- **FAMEWS maps.** Validated reports become near-real-time maps and situation overviews that support early warning, monitoring, response and risk assessment. — [IEEE GRSS](https://www.grss-ieee.org/events/fall-armyworm-monitoring-and-early-warning-system-famews/)
- **FAMEWS community data.** A community-based initiative in six East African countries collected FAMEWS field data. Pooled analysis showed infestation varied with crop phenology, high at the vegetative and reproductive stages and low at maturity. Citation: Niassy et al. (2021), *PLOS ONE* — [IITA repository](https://biblio1.iita.org/handle/20.500.12478/7167?show=full)
- **FAMEWS and satellites.** FAMEWS data from Kenya was used to validate Sentinel-2 NDVI satellite monitoring. Validation results were mixed because of clouds, intercropping, weeds and other pests. Citation: Buchaillot et al. (2022), *Remote Sensing* 14:5003 — [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-14-05003/article_deploy/remotesensing-14-05003.pdf)
- **ICT4BXW early warning.** 58,572 banana farmers received early-warning SMS alerts. — [ICT4BXW Newsletter III](https://www.iita.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ICT4BXW-Newsletter-III-Edition.pdf)
- **ICT4BXW cumulative reach.** It is cited as reaching more than 325,000 farmers. This figure is from prior work cited in the Rwanda paper. — [Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)
- **Drone + ground banana surveillance.** The Alliance framework uses YOLOv9 on aerial images and YOLOv8 on ground images, organized by where each disease occurs. — [Sci Rep 2025 / Alliance record](https://alliancebioversityciat.org/publications-data/digital-framework-georeferenced-multiplatform-surveillance-banana-wilt-using)
- **Sigatoka mapping prototype.** The 2025 Black Sigatoka smartphone IoT device georeferences infection stage and syncs to a remote host for mapping. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/00410ef5234d436ab97fd6f746a21731)
- **Sigatoka early-warning indicator.** Sigatocare produces a forward-looking, color-coded spray-need indicator. — [ITK](https://www.itk.fr/en/news/black-sigatoka-disease-of-banana-symptoms-control-and-indicators/)

### Inferences
- The team's georeferenced map plus an offline station already resembles FAMEWS and ICT4BXW in miniature. It could become a cooperative-level early-warning tool: a shared map of confirmed Sigatoka hotspots, plot-level risk colours and alerts to basic phones. The drone would cover large or hard-to-walk areas and phones would verify on the ground.
- A tiered "capture, confirm, map" pipeline keeps the shared map trustworthy, because only confirmed points are published. FAMEWS uses national focal points for the same purpose.
- Satellite and drone surveillance validated against ground reports gave mixed results in FAMEWS. Drone-derived Sigatoka maps should therefore be presented as screening layers that need ground confirmation, not as definitive diagnoses.

### Gaps
- No Latin American or Mexican community or cooperative Sigatoka surveillance programme using drones or phones was found. Mexican plant-health campaigns (SENASICA/CESAVE) were not searched.
- I could not retrieve FAMEWS user numbers or a formal usability or impact evaluation.
- No cost data (per farmer or per hectare) was found for drone-based smallholder disease surveillance.
