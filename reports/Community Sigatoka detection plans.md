# Trade Benchmark Accuracy for Field-Proven Sigatoka Triage

Platanos Machos will not win Expo Ingenierías with a higher benchmark score; the winning move is to produce the first honest field evidence for an offline Black Sigatoka triage tool that a low-resource grower group helped design and actually received. The team's **91.8% top-1** sits inside the **87–99% band** that almost every published close-up leaf classifier reports on its own held-out images, while drone systems scored per object reach only **55–90%**, and no Sigatoka study found in this research tests on Mexican plants. Deployed tools such as PlantVillage Nuru, Tumaini and ICT4BXW add a second lesson: field accuracy falls to about **65% for a single leaf**, and language, device access and extension follow-up decide continued use more than model quality does. The need in Mexico is concrete: in Chiapas and Tabasco, which together produced about 53% of national volume in 2018–2022, only **35–41% of production units monitor pests or diseases at all**, only **2–4% have credit**, and **55% of Chiapas producers are indigenous**. A shared, offline system that says "I don't know" when it should and routes hard cases to a human fills a gap no existing tool covers, and every improvement it needs is software only: calibrated confidence with an abstain path, an out-of-distribution gate, TensorRT on the Jetson, the same ONNX model in a phone web app, zone-level incidence maps and a report a grower can share on WhatsApp. Of the four plans below, the hybrid Plan D is recommended for a 1 December entry because one set of field sessions counts toward the academic, Social Impact and Entrepreneurship juries at once. The calendar is the hard constraint: if the team is registered in the Monterrey edition, the video, report and three-image profile are due **20 November at 23:59, 12 days from now**, and the Social Impact rubric scores "Solidario" at **zero** unless a vulnerable user both helped design the product and received it. One number must also change before anything is published: the old "2 M MXN" cost figure was **2 M MXN per week for the entire Tabasco plantain sector in 2013**, not a cost per plantation.

## Published Sigatoka models score 87–99% on their own data and rarely leave it

The academic literature splits into two tiers that measure different things. **Close-up phone and leaf classifiers almost always report 87–99% accuracy on a held-out split of the same dataset they trained on.** InceptionV3 to ResNet152 scored 95.41–99.2% in Tanzania ([Sanga et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.48084/etasr.3452)); BananaSqueezeNet reached 96.25% in Bangladesh ([Bhuiyan et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atech.2023.100214)); EfficientNet, ResNet and VGG reached 87–89% on 900 Ecuadorian leaves, where tenfold augmentation "did not significantly improve" accuracy ([Jiménez et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/ai6030061)); and CNN baselines hit 0.96 on 14,763 public images ([Quijije et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1861135)). The Quijije authors say what most others leave out: their result is "a methodological proof-of-concept rather than evidence of direct operational validity in Ecuadorian plantations", and CNNs "may be exploiting dataset-specific cues or backgrounds". Even a 7B vision-language model shows the gap, with BananaVLM falling from 92.21% in-domain to 83.28% out-of-domain ([arXiv 2609.25040, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25040)).

**Drone and system-level studies score lower and more honestly, because they count individual objects in real canopy.** YOLOv8 instance segmentation of Black Sigatoka and Moko from Ecuadorian UAV images reached precision 79.6%, recall 80.3% and **mAP@0.5 84.9%**, with occluded, overlapping canopy named as the failure mode ([Oviedo et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/technologies13090382)). A Colombian MobileNetV2 reached 86.5%, but on multispectral false colour rather than RGB ([Linero-Ramos et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/drones8090503)). The strongest system-level reference is CIAT/Bioversity's Tumaini framework, which combines 70,263 ground-level and about 23,584 UAV annotations with human-in-the-loop review and a georeferenced map of 70,953 plants ([Mora et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-87588-2)). Reporting by region and plant part, it gets 55–86% on aerial images for wilt classes, **0.80–0.90 mAP@0.5 for Black Sigatoka on leaves**, and only **0.60–0.71 for healthy leaves**. Leaf level is that system's weakest plant part, and its authors state that human-in-the-loop UAV surveillance for banana "remain[s] underexplored".

The training data explains why field validation is missing. The team's split (sana 3,029 / sigatoka 3,226 / otra 4,973) closely matches the CC BY 4.0 Tanzanian Banana Leaves Imagery Dataset (3,339 healthy / 3,496 Black Sigatoka / 4,932 Fusarium wilt race 1) ([Mduma & Elinisa 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04456-4); [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7670326)). Two consequences follow. The model's "otra_condicion" class mostly learned **Tanzanian Fusarium wilt** and never saw the confounders that matter in Mexico, such as nutrient deficiency, mechanical damage, senescence, Cordana and Yellow Sigatoka. And the licence requires attribution in the report and video. The rest of the public data is mostly close-ups from Bangladesh, India and Ethiopia: BananaLSD ([Arman et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2023.109608)), PSFD-Musa ([Medhi & Deb 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2022.108427)), and Mendeley sets with unverified licences. The one open low-cost **UAV RGB Black Sigatoka** dataset is Colombian (DS-v1/DS-v2, CC BY 4.0, 21.5 GB, YOLO boxes), and its companion article was not found ([Zenodo, Garcia-Braham 2026](https://zenodo.org/records/20945958)). This research found no open Mexican dataset, no dataset with Stover/Gauhl severity labels, and no peer-reviewed deep-learning study of Mexican Black Sigatoka.

Early detection and severity are thinner still. Reliable pre-symptomatic detection has been shown only with **hyperspectral** imaging of inoculated plants, at 98% ([Ugarte Fajardo et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/aps3.11383)). RGB stage work consists of a 4-stage CNN at 89% validation accuracy in a low-tier journal ([Kambo et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.17485/ijst/v17i37.2453)) and a ResNeXt50 grading result of 95.53% that is snippet-only ([IEEE 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/iatmsi60426.2024.10503074)). Human severity scoring is itself noisy: a 2024 standard area diagram (SAD) improved rater agreement over Stover's scale, under which only 38% of raters reached R² > 0.80 (snippet-only) ([Pinzón-Núñez et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10658-024-02917-x)). On-device numbers barely exist for banana models. Vendor benchmarks on a Jetson Orin Nano Super show a YOLO26 detection model at **15.76 ms in ONNX vs 4.57 ms in TensorRT FP16** with no accuracy loss, while INT8 drops mAP from 0.480 to 0.449 ([Ultralytics Jetson guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/nvidia-jetson/)). No YOLOv8-cls or banana benchmark on an Orin Nano was found, so measuring one is itself a small, citable contribution.

## Deployed tools show that people, language and follow-up matter more than accuracy

The tools that actually reached smallholders tell a consistent story. PlantVillage Nuru, a phone model for cassava, scored **65%** in the field, beating extension agents (40–58%) and farmers (18–31%); assessing **six leaves per plant raised it to 74–88%**, and an older model version scored only 30–41% on three of four phones ([Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.590889/full)). Its original paper reports F1 falling **32% on real-world images because recall collapsed**, which means diseases were missed ([Ramcharan et al. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpls.2019.00272/pdf)). Two lessons transfer directly to the team: aggregate many frames per plant or zone instead of trusting one photo, and tune for Sigatoka recall, because a missed infection costs more than a false alarm a human can dismiss.

Adoption fades once projects end, and access, not ability, explains who keeps going. In Côte d'Ivoire only about **45%** of farmers kept using Nuru, and only 23% in one department; **33% hit installation failures** and **41% found registration complex**. Continued use rose with extension-officer monitoring (+17%) and awareness campaigns (+18%) about as much as with owning a smartphone (+15%) ([Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)). In Rwanda, 85.4% of farmers said they would use Tumaini, but only **39.3% of trained farmers** did, split **26.3% of women vs 60.9% of men**. Only 12.2% of men and 6.8% of women could read any of the app's three languages, and women more often owned keypad phones (65–70%) ([Ndayizeye et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1990189/full)). ICT4BXW, a banana Xanthomonas wilt programme, added IVR, SMS and USSD because up to 60% of Rwandan farmers use basic phones, and by its own report then reached 89,707 callers and 58,572 SMS alert recipients ([ICT4BXW Newsletter III](https://www.iita.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/ICT4BXW-Newsletter-III-Edition.pdf)).

No RCT was found that links any AI diagnosis app to yield, pesticide use or income. The best causal evidence covers general mobile agricultural advice, which raised yields by about **4%** and the odds of adopting a recommended input by about **22%** ([Fabregas, Kremer & Schilbach 2019, Science, doi:10.1126/science.aay3038](https://precisiondev.org/?p=756)). Plantix shows the governance risk: it passed 20M downloads and then pivoted toward selling agrochemicals ([Agrisnip 2025](https://agrisnip.com/asafal/plantixs-pivot-from-pesticide-reduction-to-agrochemical-sales-and-a)). Sigatoka-specific tools are niche. Sigatocare (ITK/CIRAD) gives a colour-coded spray-need indicator ([ITK](https://www.itk.fr/en/news/black-sigatoka-disease-of-banana-symptoms-control-and-indicators/)); SIGAPLANT makes unevaluated "first-stage" AI claims ([SIGAPLANT](https://www.sigaplant.com/)); a Colombian system characterized its users before building its classifier ([Escudero et al. 2021](https://scielo.br/j/rbf/a/mGgTrgCWkrdxLmvRBtrDmMx/?lang=en)); and a 2025 smartphone IoT prototype maps georeferenced infection stages ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/00410ef5234d436ab97fd6f746a21731)). **None is documented with smallholders in Chiapas or Tabasco.**

In working deployments the AI is a triage aid inside a human network. FAO's FAMEWS has national focal points validate farmer reports before they reach the shared map ([IEEE GRSS](https://www.grss-ieee.org/events/fall-armyworm-monitoring-and-early-warning-system-famews/)), and Tumaini routes predictions through expert human-in-the-loop review ([Mora et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-87588-2)). Using an app does not train its users: two weeks of Nuru left extension workers' diagnostic skill at 49.9% before and 50.5% after ([Mrisho et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2020.590889/full)). The team's station already matches this pattern, with an uncertainty-first review queue, a held-out field exam, model versioning and a georeferenced map. What it lacks is the human network around it and a phone path for growers.

## Mexican smallholders skip the monitoring that export plantations do weekly

Mexico grows bananas and plantains on more than **87,000 ha in 16 states** and harvests about **2.6 Mt a year** ([Portal Frutícola, Jan 2026, citing SADER/SENASICA](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=609837)). Chiapas (28.1%), Tabasco (24.9%) and Veracruz (12.9%) led the 2018–2022 average ([Portal Frutícola, Apr 2024, citing SIAP](https://www.portalfruticola.com/noticias/2024/04/18/norteamerica-y-japon-son-los-principales-consumidores-de-platano-mexicano)). INEGI's 2022 agricultural census counts more area than SIAP: 30,701 ha and 804,216 t in Tabasco, and 31,623 ha and 592,707 t in Chiapas ([INEGI Tabasco](https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/saladeprensa/boletines/2023/CA_Def/CA_Def2022_Tab.pdf); [INEGI Chiapas](https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/saladeprensa/boletines/2023/CA_Def/CA_Def2022_Chis.pdf)). The census includes mixed and backyard plots, so the difference probably reflects small, scattered growers that commercial statistics undercount, who are exactly the project's users. The census producer profile covers all crops, not only banana, but it describes the target user well.

|---|---|---|
| Producers self-identifying as indigenous | 27% | **55%** (86.7% of them speak an indigenous language) |
| Production units with credit / insurance | 2.3% / 0.7% | 4% / 0.5% |

The disease pressure is severe. Uncontrolled Black Sigatoka cuts yield **35–50%** ([ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)), CICY puts the range at 40–100% ([Diario de Yucatán 2018](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)), and Chiapas border producers reported losses of **up to 40%** in 2021 ([SENASICA Monitor Fitosanitario 2021](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/Contenido/files/2021/junio/MonitorFitosanitario16062021_a6a0f3b3-26b8-45cd-b974-bfd014d2c182.pdf)). Commercial fields count as "intensive" above **25 sprays a year** ([Braz. J. Microbiol. 2014](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/en/lil-709492)), and Tabasco reports 48–52 (snippet-only, [Agrociencia summary](https://es.scribd.com/document/693914784/Agroquimicos)). Resistance is a shared problem. Mexican isolates from intensive fields had mean azoxystrobin EC50 of **51.58 mg/L vs 13.25 mg/L** in rustic, unsprayed fields, and propiconazole 10.01 vs 1.225 mg/L. Resistance indicators also appeared in rustic-field strains, "suggesting that proximity among fields may be causing a fungus interchange" ([Braz. J. Microbiol. 2014](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/en/lil-709492)). Smallholders inherit resistance bred by their neighbours' spraying, which makes a shared community disease map that supports coordinated stewardship a public good, not just a private tool.

**Correct the impact numbers before anything is published.** The "2 M MXN" claim most likely comes from a 2013 Fundación Produce Tabasco statement that fighting Black Sigatoka cost **2 million pesos per week for the whole Tabasco sector**, about **100 million pesos a year** or **35% of production costs**, with about 17,000 ha planted and 2,000 ha affected. The FreshPlaza headline says "dollars" while the article body says pesos ([FreshPlaza 2013](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/2110011/mexico-banana-producers-lose-100-million-dollars-a-year/)). Dividing gives about **5,900 MXN/ha/yr in 2013 pesos**; that is our calculation, not a published figure. A 2003-era source gives about **US$1,100/ha/yr** in fungicides (snippet-only, [UACH TecnoCiencia](https://revistascientificas.uach.mx/index.php/tecnociencia/article/download/594/2491/11419)). The line "25–30% of production cost" in `docs/11-producto-negocio.md` has no support in the notes. Sources give **35%** (Tabasco 2013), **about 50%** (CICY 2018, [Diario de Yucatán](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)) and about 60% in one unopened quote (snippet-only, [Portal Frutícola](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=20910)). Always present a sourced range with years. The most useful impact benchmark is also not the team's: **a CICY-led project cut fungicide use by up to 40% in Colima and 20% in Tabasco** through better monitoring and management ([Diario de Yucatán 2018](https://www.yucatan.com.mx/merida/2018/01/24/menor-dano-a-cultivos.html)). Use it as the outcome better monitoring makes possible, never as a result of this tool.

Standard monitoring is manual leaf reading by trained technicians every week, using Stover's youngest-leaf-spotted count and the biological forewarning protocol on young leaves, and first chlorotic specks appear 14–20 days after infection ([ProMusa](https://www.promusa.org/Black+leaf+streak)). SENASICA has adopted drones, but for a different disease: drone surveillance over **4,447 ha** from February 2025, satellites over 4,400 ha from July 2025, and **667,348 plants** lab-tested ([Portal Frutícola, Jan 2026](https://www.portalfruticola.com/?p=609837)). The target is Fusarium wilt TR4 (Foc R4T), which SENASICA classifies as **absent from Mexico**; suspected cases go to the CNRF for PCR confirmation through the hotline (800) 98-79-879 ([SENASICA Aviso Foc R4T, Feb 2026](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/contenido/docs/vgtl/foc_r4t/Aviso_Epidemiologico_Foc_R4T_13022026.pdf)). No Sigatoka-specific federal or state campaign was found. Black Sigatoka has been under a permanent internal quarantine since 1987, meant to keep it out of regions not yet affected ([SENASICA 2021](https://dj.senasica.gob.mx/Contenido/files/2021/junio/MonitorFitosanitario16062021_a6a0f3b3-26b8-45cd-b974-bfd014d2c182.pdf)). For the stand, that means **do not carry infected leaves across states**: use photos, prints or locally sourced healthy leaves, and check with SENASICA before moving any plant material. A 59-year climate analysis for Teapa, Tabasco finds **October and March** the most favourable months for the disease ([Agroproductividad 2024](https://revista-agroproductividad.org/index.php/agroproductividad/article/view/2500)), so November is a reasonable month to find visible symptoms. The likely partners already work on the problem: CICY (Dra. Blondy Canto Canché) and its INIFAP, UJAT, UACh, U. de Colima and UAG network; Colegio de Postgraduados Campus Tabasco; SENASICA's state plant-health committees (Organismos Auxiliares de Sanidad Vegetal); Sistema Producto Plátano technicians; Fundación Produce Tabasco; and the Asociación de Plataneros del Soconusco (snippet-only).

## The gap the project can own is honest, offline, community-run triage

None of the obvious novelty claims survives the literature. The drone, YOLO and web-map combination already exists in Ecuador ([AI-BananaMapping, CCIS 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-08366-1_14)); georeferenced human-in-the-loop surveillance exists at CGIAR scale (Mora 2025); phone diagnosis exists in Tumaini; "drone plus AI detects crop disease" is a recurring student project worldwide, as UDSM, UCU's Crop Saver and Bahrain's AeroDoctor show in the Expo notes; and a Tec Expo has already awarded AI classification, for scrap identification in 2019 ([Conecta Tec 2019](https://conecta.tec.mx/es/noticias/monterrey/educacion/premia-tec-la-innovacion-y-el-emprendimiento-en-la-ingenieria)).

What nobody has published is an **independent evaluation of a public-data Sigatoka model on Mexican plants**, scored against blind expert judgments and split by farm and date. Nor has anyone deployed an **offline, Spanish-first triage tool that a cooperative or ejido runs on shared hardware**, with abstention and tiered human confirmation built in. The team's design answers several documented failure modes directly. Running offline on a local station removes the install and connectivity failures that hit Nuru. A browser app served from the station needs no account or app-store install. A shared device works around the smartphone-ownership gap that excluded women in Rwanda. And local-first data that the community owns answers both the Plantix capture risk and the CARE principles for Indigenous data governance ([CARE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARE_Principles_for_Indigenous_Data_Governance)), while fitting Mexico's new personal-data law, in force since 21 March 2025 ([Garrigues](https://www.garrigues.com/es_ES/noticia/mexico-nueva-ley-federal-proteccion-datos-personales-posesion-particulares-introduce)). Smaller technical contributions sit alongside: the first Jetson Orin Nano benchmark of a banana classifier, a lab-to-field drop measured with confidence intervals, and a learning curve showing how fast reviewed field images close that drop. Over a longer horizon the team could release the first open, georeferenced Mexican Sigatoka dataset with SAD-assisted severity labels.

One strategic point follows from the adoption evidence and from scenario B in the team's own `docs/11-producto-negocio.md`. **For low-resource growers, the phone path is the one that scales.** The drone becomes a shared screening asset for large or hard-to-walk plots, and phones confirm on the ground. That framing costs nothing in hardware, because the PWA runs the same ONNX file, and it lets the system answer the question the adoption literature says judges should ask: who can use this without us?

## Four plans that change only the model and the ground station

All four plans keep the drone, Jetson, ZED X Mini, Pixhawk and GPS exactly as they are. Every change lands in the classifier and its export, the inference path in `jetson/zed_right.py`, or the Flask ground station and its pages. Effort figures come from the technical notes where available; figures marked "est." are this report's own estimates. This plan answers the academic jury's two hardest questions, what is new this semester and whether the system works robustly and consistently, by turning the literature's biggest weakness, missing independent validation, into the team's headline result. ## Claims that would cost credibility with any jury

The fastest way to lose the academic jury's trust, and the Social Impact jury's points for measured impact, is a claim the literature contradicts. The video, report, poster and pitch should use the replacements below.

|---|---|---|
| "The system is 91.8% accurate." | "91.8% top-1 on a public close-up test set, likely Tanzanian, whose third class is Fusarium wilt. On [n] Mexican field images it scored [x]% (95% CI [a–b])." | Lab-to-field drops reach 99%→31% ([Mohanty 2016](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2016.01419)), and the public set's HEALTHY labels are noisy (`vision/train/experiments.md`). |

Several further limitations should be stated openly rather than discovered by a judge. The evidence covers one season and a small n, with expert labels from one or two raters. Flights need a qualified pilot, and the camera link has shown intermittent CSI CRC errors. Confounders such as nutrient deficiency, Cordana and Yellow Sigatoka are not yet modelled. The weather-risk idea in the longer horizon is an "índice de condiciones favorables", not a validated forecast, because only one Mexican climate study supports it ([Agroproductividad 2024](https://revista-agroproductividad.org/index.php/agroproductividad/article/view/2500)). ## Conclusion

The research changes what counts as the project's main asset. It is no longer the 91.8% number but the gap between that number and what the system scores on Mexican plants, measured honestly and then closed step by step through the review queue the team has already built. That turns the expected field drop from a liability into the contribution the literature says is missing. Because the Social Impact and Entrepreneurship juries reuse the academic score and judge in person, the same calibrated, abstaining, field-tested core helps in all three juries, and the deciding resource for the next 12 days is not GPU time or code but a named grower group that agrees to shape and receive the tool.

The second shift is in what success means for a low-resource community. Some of Nuru's users stopped because they had learned to recognize the disease themselves ([Adjéi et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2024.1433204/full)), and fungicide resistance spreads between neighbouring fields ([Braz. J. Microbiol. 2014](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/en/lil-709492)). The most defensible long-term impact claim is therefore a community that monitors more often, sprays more selectively and needs the app less over time, with the drone as a shared screening layer and the phone and the human reviewer doing the work that scales. If the team builds the next edition, the DSS follow-up year and a Premio Eugenio Garza Sada entry around those measurable behaviours instead of model accuracy, the project has a clear path beyond 1 December.
